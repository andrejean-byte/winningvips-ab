Responsible Gaming

Gambling should always be a form of entertainment, never a source of stress. We are committed to promoting safe and responsible play.

Gambling Should Be Entertainment, Not Income

It is important to understand that gambling is designed to be entertainment. The odds are structured so that the house maintains an edge over time. While individual sessions can result in wins, gambling should never be relied upon as a source of income or a way to solve financial problems.

Before you play, set a budget you are comfortable losing entirely. If you win, consider it a bonus. If you lose, accept it as the cost of entertainment and walk away. Never chase losses by increasing your bets or extending your playing time beyond what you originally planned.

Warning Signs of Problem Gambling

Problem gambling can develop gradually and may not be immediately obvious. If you recognize any of the following behaviors in yourself or someone you know, it may be time to seek help:

  • Spending more money or time on gambling than you can afford
  • Chasing losses by placing larger or more frequent bets
  • Borrowing money or selling possessions to fund gambling
  • Neglecting work, studies, family, or personal responsibilities due to gambling
  • Feeling anxious, irritable, or restless when trying to reduce or stop gambling
  • Lying to friends or family about the extent of your gambling activity
  • Using gambling as a way to escape problems or relieve feelings of stress, guilt, or depression
  • Repeated unsuccessful attempts to control, cut back, or stop gambling
  • Risking or losing significant relationships, employment, or educational opportunities because of gambling
  • Relying on others to provide money to relieve desperate financial situations caused by gambling

If any of these apply to you, please consider reaching out to one of the organizations listed below. There is no shame in seeking help, and support is available.

Self-Exclusion Tools

Most reputable online casinos offer self-exclusion programs that allow you to temporarily or permanently block yourself from accessing your account. Self-exclusion is one of the most effective tools available for managing problem gambling behavior.

How to Self-Exclude

  • Contact the casino directly: Log in to your account and navigate to the responsible gambling section (usually found in account settings or the help center). Most casinos allow you to set a self-exclusion period ranging from 24 hours to permanent.
  • Use a third-party blocking tool: Services like GamStop (UK), BetBlocker (international), and Gamban provide software that blocks access to gambling websites across all your devices.
  • Contact your payment provider: Some banks and payment processors allow you to block transactions to gambling merchants. Check with your bank or e-wallet provider for available options.

Setting Limits

In addition to self-exclusion, we recommend using the following limits that most casinos offer:

  • Deposit Limits: Set a daily, weekly, or monthly cap on how much you can deposit.
  • Loss Limits: Set a maximum amount you are willing to lose within a given period.
  • Session Time Limits: Set a timer that alerts you or logs you out after a specified duration.
  • Wagering Limits: Set a cap on the total amount you can wager per day, week, or month.
  • Cooling-Off Periods: Take a temporary break from gambling for a set number of days or weeks.

Tips for Staying in Control

Responsible gambling starts with awareness and self-discipline. Here are practical tips to help you stay in control:

  • Set a budget before you play. Decide how much you are willing to spend and treat it as an entertainment expense. Once the budget is gone, stop playing.
  • Set a time limit. Decide in advance how long you will play. Use session time reminders offered by the casino.
  • Never chase losses. If you are on a losing streak, walk away. Increasing your bets to recover losses almost always leads to bigger losses.
  • Do not gamble under the influence. Alcohol and other substances impair judgment and can lead to reckless betting decisions.
  • Take regular breaks. Step away from the screen periodically. Prolonged sessions can cloud your judgment.
  • Balance gambling with other activities. Gambling should be one of many leisure activities, not the only one. Maintain hobbies, social connections, and routines.
  • Never borrow money to gamble. If you cannot afford to play with your own disposable income, do not play.
  • Be honest with yourself. Regularly assess whether gambling is still fun for you. If it starts feeling like a compulsion rather than a choice, seek help.
